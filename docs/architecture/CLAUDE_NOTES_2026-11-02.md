## Coding-model experiment: qwen2.5-coder:14b against Study (2026-11-04)
Grey agreed to try a coding model for the SE's code slot ("yes try it", then "send me an extensive report on it"). Downloaded qwen2.5-coder:14b (9.0 GB) and tested it against Study (qwen3:14b) and Seek (qwen3.5:9b) in non-thinking mode, the way `run_local` calls them. Report: the Claude Doc "Coding Model Experiment: qwen2.5-coder 14B vs Study". The test is now in the repo as `scripts/model_bench/` (README there) so any model can be rerun in about five minutes.
- Pilot, 10 Python tasks, one try each: coder 8, Study 7, Seek 8. Too small: on the 28 tasks added later the order reversed (coder 68 of 84 runs, Study 70, Seek 70).
- Test: 38 tasks (30 Python, 8 TypeScript; 29 new code, 9 bug fixes), 3 runs each, 114 runs per model. A reference solution passes every task and every buggy input fails its tests. Pass rate: coder 81.6%, Study 80.7%, Seek 83.3%. Mean seconds per task: 1.52, 1.67, 2.14 (Seek's total is stretched by two parsing tasks that took up to 47 s; its mean on the other 36 is 1.32 s, the fastest). Resampling the tasks: no pair is separable (95% ranges about +-10 points).
- Shape of the misses: a model's miss on a task usually repeats (coder 35 of 38 tasks gave the same result in all three runs, Study 36, Seek 31); the models miss different tasks (a single model's three runs solved 32 to 35 of 38 tasks at least once; coder + Study together 36; all three 38); 56 of the 62 failed runs ran and returned a wrong answer (formats, parsing edge cases), only 6 crashed or ignored an instruction. TypeScript: coder 16 of 24 runs, Study 19, Seek 18 (8 tasks, a question mark). Bug fixes: coder and Seek 27 of 27, Study 24 (all misses `dedupe`).
